What the fly actually sees

Its whole eye: a few hundred blurry dots. Everything it learns, it learns from this.

How it works

  1. A picture appears on the screen in front of the fly.
  2. Its eye turns it into a few hundred blurry dots, and fixed "LC" eye cells (modelled on real fly neurons) pick out edges, spots and motion.
  3. A small brain of 128 cells turns that into a decision, and the fly presses a foot.
  4. Right answers earn points; wrong ones hurt that foot. Training changes the brain so it earns more points.

Quick learn changes the connections behind the feet as the fly plays, from rewards and pain alone. Evolve breeds a population of brains and keeps the best. Both use no backpropagation - everything runs in this browser tab.

3bOr train a swarm

A swarm is many flies, each looking at the photos from its own spot, that vote on every answer. A swarm only helps if its flies see differently, so each one gets its own centre of gaze on a circle (and the sharp centre is switched on). On the face photos, one fly scores about 60%, a swarm of 9 to 25 about 65-66%, and a swarm of 25 that trains in rounds, each round practising on the photos the swarm still gets wrong ("boosting"), about 68-69%. Boosting only pays off from about 15 flies on.
