How does it find the way?

Like an ant. While the route is flown a few times, every view it sees marks the memory-centre cells it uses as familiar; nothing about the route itself is stored, only what the world looked like while on it (one bit per cell). Learning flights from all over the map toward the route fill a second memory, the way ants make learning walks around their nest. To navigate, it looks in 9 directions: until the route itself looks familiar it follows the second memory, which leads to the route; then it follows the route memory to the goal. A swarm adds up every member's opinion. When nothing looks familiar, it casts like a moth that lost a scent: sweeping side to side, wider each time, until something familiar comes into view.
